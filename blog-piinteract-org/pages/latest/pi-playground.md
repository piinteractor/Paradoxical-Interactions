„αἰὼν παῖς ἐστι παίζων, πεσσεύων· παιδὸς ἡ βασιληίη." "Time is a child at play, moving pieces on a board. The kingdom belongs to the child." — Heraclitus, Fragment 52

PI Play Ground

Every framework builds an archive: documented cases, named actors, verified sources. This is not the archive.

This is where PI goes to play.

The analyses on this blog follow rules. Every claim is grounded in observable reality. Every post cites primary sources, live-verified before publication. The archive earns its credibility case by case — that is its job, and it does not get to take a day off.

The Playground suspends exactly one rule: the requirement of evidence. What remains is the structure itself, applied where no case has been documented — or where none can be. Does PI have a lower bound, or is a single person over time already a colliding system? Are "what deserves observation" and "who deserves to observe" two questions, or one loop wearing two masks? Hypotheses. Marked as hypotheses. Played through to the end, without a net.

Why allow this at all? Because a framework that only processes what confirms it becomes the thing it describes. The archive proves. The Playground risks. Per Errorem ad Astra needs a place where the error is permitted to happen — before anyone arrives to correct it.

The playground has rules of its own. A hypothesis is named as one, in the text, not in the fine print. Nothing here claims proof. When reality volunteers a case, the case is welcome — but it serves the hypothesis, not the other way around. And whatever breaks here, breaks cheaply.

Some of what plays here will graduate to the archive. Most of it won't. That is not failure. The dandelion doesn't know where it lands. Neither does the child moving the pieces. The game is all about the game.

All are guilty. None are at fault.
